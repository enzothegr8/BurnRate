NASA has a moon base program. What it has signed, so far, is a delivery
schedule.

The program of record is <Figure factId="moonbase.program.total" /> over
<Figure factId="moonbase.program.duration_years" /> years. Neither number
appears in a NASA document. Both came out of a single sentence, spoken from a
podium at the Ignition event this spring, and every printed version of them
traces back to that sentence. The dashed line under those figures is not a
hedge. It is the provenance, stated in the type: credible, repeated, and
never published by the agency it is attributed to.

The signed record is shorter. Seven awards, worth
<Figure factId="moonbase.committed.base" /> in base value, or
<Figure factId="moonbase.committed.total" /> if you count an option period
that has not been exercised. The press mostly counts it. The distance between
those two figures is the difference between money that is committed and money
that is conditional, and that is not a rounding question. It is the whole
discipline of reading a contract announcement.

Do the division. <Figure factId="moonbase.committed.share" /> of the spoken
program is signed, and that share is the generous version, because its
numerator includes the option. A publication that wants the base to look
funded quotes the pledge. A publication that wants it to look doomed quotes
the gap. The honest read is that the gap is normal for a program this young,
and that the interesting question is not the size of the gap but what the
signed money is buying.

## What the signed money is buying

Robots. All of it.

Three lander awards landed at the end of June:
<Figure factId="moonbase.award.astrobotic.2026-06-30" /> to Astrobotic for
two deliveries, <Figure factId="moonbase.award.intuitive-machines.2026-06-30" />
to Intuitive Machines for one, and
<Figure factId="moonbase.award.firefly.lander.2026-06-30" /> to Firefly for
one. Before that, the rovers: <Figure factId="moonbase.award.lunar-outpost.2026-05-26" />
to Lunar Outpost and <Figure factId="moonbase.award.astrolab.2026-05-26" />
to Astrolab, each for a crewed lunar terrain vehicle. Blue Origin took
<Figure factId="moonbase.award.blue-origin.2026-05-26" /> in base value to
deliver those rovers to the surface, with the option period covering the
delivery missions themselves. And Firefly holds a separate
<Figure factId="moonbase.award.firefly.moonfall.2026-05-26" /> subcontract
from JPL to fly survey drones to the south pole.

Every line on that list is a robot, or a ride for one. That is not a
criticism. It is a sequence. You scout before you build, and the scouting is
what a first phase is for. But a sequence is not a base, and the branding is
running well ahead of the ledger.

## The zeros

The committed figure for pressurized habitats is
<Figure factId="moonbase.committed.habitat" />. The committed figure for
surface power is <Figure factId="moonbase.committed.power" />.

The dotted lines under those zeros mean the count is ours. Nobody publishes
an absence, so no source can be cited for it; Burn Rate computed each one by
summing an empty category of its own award ledger. They are the only numbers
on this site for which this publication is the primary source, and they are
the two most important numbers in the program. A base is a building with a
power bill. Until money flows to habitats and power, the base is a scouting
mission with excellent branding.

One of the zeros is already moving. A directed solicitation for surface
power opened at the end of June, and habitation is listed as the follow-on
topic behind it. A solicitation is not an award, but it is the paperwork that
precedes one, which makes surface power the single most useful thing to
watch in this program right now.

## The per-person number

Spread the spoken total across the country and across the window, and the
program prices out at about <Figure factId="moonbase.cost_per_person" /> per
American per year. That figure is dotted twice over. The denominator is a
choice, because resident population, tax returns, and net positive taxpayers
each give a different answer. And the window came out of the same podium
sentence as the total, so the divisor inherits every doubt the headline
figure carries. It is a useful number for scale and a useless one for
budgeting, which is true of most per-person numbers.

## What would change this piece

A habitat award. A power award. Either one flips a zero, and because the
zeros are computed from the ledger rather than typed into this page, they
will flip here the same day, in the same commit. It has been
<Figure factId="moonbase.days_since_award" /> days since the last award
landed, and the cadence so far suggests the next one is a matter of weeks.

A budget is a promise, and promises are not receipts. The money so far
grades the road. The building has not been ordered.
